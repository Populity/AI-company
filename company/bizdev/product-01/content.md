**Walt's rule:** Name a backup person only after asking them. Surprising a colleague is not a handover. It is a haunting.

## 8. PER MY LAST MEMO: THE FOLLOW-UP GENERATOR

**Walt says:** *There are four stages of following up. Stage one is polite. Stage four is a small, tidy sigh in writing. Pick your stage. Be kind. Remember that the person you are writing to is also tired.*

**Pick one item from each column, or write your own.**

| Opening | The Reminder | The Closing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. "Hope your week is going well." | A. "I'm checking in on ________." | X. "Thank you for your time." |
| 2. "Just floating this back up." | B. "Do you have an update on ________?" | Y. "No rush. (A small rush.)" |
| 3. "Circling back, in the good sense." | C. "I wanted to make sure ________ didn't slip." | Z. "I'll check again on ________." |
| 4. "Per my last memo." | D. "As noted in my previous message, ________." | W. "Noted. Always, noted." |

**Ready-made examples**

*Stage 1, Gentle:*
"Hi ________, hope your week is going well. I'm checking in on ________. No rush. Thank you for your time."

*Stage 2, Clear:*
"Hi ________, just floating this back up. Do you have an update on ________? I'll check again on ________."

*Stage 3, Firm:*
"Hi ________, circling back. I wanted to make sure ________ didn't slip. I'd appreciate a reply by ________."

*Stage 4, Walt:*
"Hello ________. Per my last memo, ________. Noted. Always, noted."

**Walt's warning:** Never write "As per my previous email" in all capital letters. In 1987 we reserved capital letters for the word URGENT, and then we lost the stamp for it.
